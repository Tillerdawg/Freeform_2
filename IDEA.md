We are building an open-source, browser-based, drill-design tool for marching bands and drum corps, called "Freeform". This tool would be accompanied by a phone/tablet (ios/android) app that allows individual performers to see the drill movements from the director's perspective and from their personal perspective. Show creators/drill writers must be able to place performers on a football field (default), basketball court, or other custom surface; define sets; animate transitions across counts; and export drill charts and coordinate sheets as PDFs and to the performer app. A football field must be configured to accept high-school, college/drum corps, or NFL hash marks. For American football fields, Side 1 is defined as the left side of the 50 yard line from the perspective of the director. Side 2 is defined as the right side of the 50 yard line from the perspective of the director. The basic step size should be 8-to-5 (8 steps to 5 yards), meaning that one basic standard marching step is 22 and 1/2 inches in length.

Drill writers must be able to place performers (individually or in groups) on a marching surface (as dots, letters, or icons) into different formations, called "sets" or "pages." To do this, tyipcally, they are presented with a digital football field overlayed with a grid of 8-to-5 steps starting at the front sideline, stretching the length and width of the field. Transitions between sets would take a drill-writer-defined number of counts. Drill-writers will be able to include text and make some mark-up on individual pages, e.g., to give performers the number of counts in a movement, measure numbers, body position, horn angle, arrows for direction of movement on a follow-the-leader type move, etc. Drill-writers should be able to scrub and play individual sets, sub-collections of sets, or the whole collection of sets, called a "show," as an animated timeline. Individual performers should be able to scrub and play individual sets, a sub-collection of sets, or the entire show as an animated timeline from the director's perspective and from their own perspective.

Drill-writers should also be able to sync music files (mp3, midi, etc.) to the show so that they, ensemble directors, and performers and see a more complete show in digital form so that they can more easily translate that digital show to real 3-D space.

Importantly, drill-writers will need to be able to place performers into lines, arcs, curviliniear, free, and other shapes easily. If the distance between two sets for a given performer is larger than a 6-to-5 (6 steps to 5 yards), based on the number of counts between those two sets, the drill-writer should be notified in some way. Pyware handles this notification by turning the performer's path between sets red and showing the stepsize in red text in the timeline. As a rule of thumb, the largest step size is a 4-to-5 (4 steps to 5 yards). Since a performer may have the same drill coordinate for two or more consecutive sets, there is no smallest step size.

Drill coordinates are specified in standard 8-to-5 steps away from the closest field markings. For a given performer, the drill coordinates are given in the following format: Side-to-side yardline relationship then Front-to-back relationship from the front sideline, front hash, back hash, back sideline, depending on which one is closer to the coordinate. For example, if a performer's coordinate for a particular set places them 45 inches to the right of the side 2 45 yard line and 45 inches in front of the front hash mark, the performer's coordinate would be "2 steps outside the Side 2 45, 2 steps in front of Front Hash." "Outside" a yard line means the performer is placed on the side of a given yardline that is further away from the 50 yardline. "Inside" a yardline means the performer is placed on the side of a given yardline that is closer to the 50 yardline. If a performer is placed exactly between two yardlines, for example between the 40 and 45 on Side 1, the coordinate would be "Splitting 40 and 45 on Side 1." Because the 50 yardline is the boundary between Side 1 and Side 2, if a performer is placed on the 50 yardline, there is no Side designation for that position. If, for a given set, a performer is placed near the 50 yardline, that performer's coordinate would be "outside the 50" on Side 1 or Side 2. It is not possible to have a coordinate "inside the 50."

Drill writers should have the ability to set certain transitions between sets for an arbitrary number of performers as "float" (default) or "follow-the-leader" or "FTL." If a transition is set to FTL, the  drill writer should be able to specify the "line leader" and the specify the order of the selected performers. In an FTL movement, performers move in order toward the leader through the field positions of the preceeding performers throughout the movement. For example, there are 5 performers (A,B,C,D, and E) placed on front hashmark the field spaced evenly side to side between the 40 on Side 1 to the 50, where Performer A starts on the 40, Performer B is splitting the 40 and 45, Performer C is on the 45, Performer D is splitting the 45 and 50, and Performer E is on the 50. For a 16 count move, Performer A takes 16 8-to-5 steps directly forward, toward the front sideline. Performer B would take 4 steps left toward Performer A's starting position, then take 12 steps forward. Performer C would take 8 steps left, then 8 steps forward. Performer D would take 12 steps left, then 4 steps forward. Performer E would take 16 counts left. The result is that all the performers are now in a straight line on the Side 1 40 yardline, spaced 4 steps apart front to back.

Drill writers and performers should be able to play back a given movement/transition-between-sets on a count by count basis, pausing on any count needed to gain clarity for the movement.

The ideas for these tools are based on Pyware (https://www.pyware.com/) and the Ultimate Drill Book (UDB) App (https://www.ultimatedrillbook.com/udbapp/). The goal for this project is to remove Pyware's/UDB's licensing cost as a barrier for school and community programs.
