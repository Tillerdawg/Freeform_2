FreeFORM Drill: Drill, without the bill.

FreeFORM Drill is a free drill design/teaching tool for marching band, drum corps, and indoor marching ensembles that runs in your web browser. Write the show on a computer or an iPad, watch every move count by count with your music, print dot sheets and drill charts, and send the drill to your students' phones. There's nothing to install, no account to make, and no license fee, for an ensemble of any size.

On a phone, FreeFORM shows the drill the way your performers see it. To write drill, open this page on a computer or an iPad.

For directors and drill writers

  • High school, college, or NFL hashes, a basketball court, or a custom floor, with coordinates in standard drill language ("2 steps outside the Side 2 45, 2 steps in front of Front Hash").
  • Lines, arcs, curves, circles, blocks, stars, spirals, and letters, with spots assigned by least total travel so paths don't cross.
  • Follow the leader, counter-marches, custom paths, delayed step-offs, and turning moves, all at one even step size.
  • Step-size and too-close warnings, and one Review list of every problem in the show.
  • Play it back with your MuseScore score and recording, or tap the counts along with any mp3.
  • Dot sheets and drill charts for the whole show or a few sets, printed or saved as PDF.
  • Every tool from the start. No tiers and no performer limits.

For students and staff

Students scan a QR code (or open a link you text them), pick who they are, and see their dot: where they stand now, where they go next, how many counts it takes, and how big the steps are. They can watch any move from the press box or facing the front, loop a few sets with a count-in, and it all works offline once it's opened.

Coming from Pyware or UDB

FreeFORM works the way you're used to: sets and counts, an 8-to-5 grid seen from the press box, Side 1 and Side 2, and coordinates read from the nearest yard line and hash. Most of it will feel familiar.

  • Bring your shows. FreeFORM opens Pyware 3D files (.3dj): sets, counts, notes, measures, sections, and roster names. It's been tested with files from 2008 to 2010, so newer files may not come in cleanly yet. If one doesn't, please tell me.
  • What FreeFORM doesn't do yet: a 3D view, cloud sync between computers, and app-store apps. Your shows are kept in your browser and in files you save.
  • FreeFORM isn't affiliated with Pyware or Ultimate Drill Book. It was written from scratch, from how those programs behave and what their documentation says, with none of their code.

Privacy

FreeFORM has no accounts, and the drill you write never leaves your browser unless you share it.

  • Your shows are saved in your own browser, and in the files you save. They aren't sent anywhere.
  • There are no ads, no trackers, and no analytics on this site.
  • The feedback form goes to a small FreeFORM service that emails it to me. It isn't stored or used for anything else, and your email address (if you give one) is only for writing back. The service sees your internet address for a moment, to stop anyone sending floods of messages, and doesn't keep it.
  • Students' names are left out of plain share links, since anyone with the link can open it.
  • When you publish a show to phones, it's encrypted in your browser before it's uploaded, and the key is only in your link (the part after the #, which browsers never send to any server). FreeFORM's service stores only the encrypted file: no one can read it without your link, not even me. Shows nobody opens for a year are deleted.
  • If you publish to your own GitHub repository instead, your token stays in your browser.
  • The site itself is served by GitHub Pages, which keeps the usual web server logs, like any website.

Free and open source

I'm Jason Tiller, a former band director. I wrote shows in Pyware for years, and the yearly license, the performer limits, and the tools locked behind higher tiers wore on me. FreeFORM is my answer: every tool from the start, for any size band, free, and it always will be. If it saves your program money, you can buy me a coffee to help with hosting, but you never have to. It's open source under the MIT license, so anyone can use it, share it, and change it.

I work at the University of Georgia, but FreeFORM Drill is a personal project. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by the University of Georgia, and I don't speak for the University here.

FreeFORM is new, and I'm working on it actively. If something doesn't work the way a drill writer would expect, I'd like to hear about it.
